How to Play

Everything you need to throw your first stone — no curling experience required. This covers modes, controls, the basic rules, and how to get a match going with friends.

01Modes & formats

When you start a match, you'll choose two things: whether you're playing Single Player or Multiplayer, and whether you're playing Standard or Doubles.

Standard

4 players per team (lead, second, vice, skip). Each team throws 8 stones per end, 2 per player. The classic format.

Doubles

2 players per team. Each end starts with one stone already placed for each side, then each player throws — a faster, more open game.

In Single Player, you play every position on your team yourself, against a full team of AI opponents. In Multiplayer, any open slots on either team — including your own if you're short a player — can be filled with AI, so you never need a full eight people to play.

02Getting oriented

Curling sheets are long, so CurlingOne gives you a few ways to get around without walking the full length of the ice in your living room.

Double-click X or Y
Teleport between the two ends of the sheet.
X (single click)
Toggle auto-follow, so your view tracks a moving stone automatically.
Y
Fast-forward while stones are in motion.

03Delivering a stone

In short: grab the stone, aim with your thumbstick, pull the trigger to slide, and twist as you let go to curl it.

  1. Grab the stone with your grip trigger from the hack.
  2. Aim and set weight using your thumbstick — side to side adjusts your target line, forward and back sets how much weight (speed) you're putting behind the shot.
  3. Slide by pulling and releasing the index trigger, which starts your physical delivery down the ice.
  4. Release the grip trigger mid-slide to let go of the stone. The direction you're twisting your controller as you release determines the curl — turning clockwise curls the stone to the right, counterclockwise curls it left.

04Sweeping

Once a stone is moving, anyone on your team can sweep in front of it to reduce friction and help it travel farther or straighter. Grab your broom with either hand's grip trigger, then move it back and forth just ahead of the stone — the faster and more consistently you sweep, the more effect it has.

05Scoring & the basic rules

If you've never watched curling, here's the short version: two teams take turns sliding stones toward a circular target (the house), trying to end up closest to the center. After both teams have thrown all their stones for an end, whoever is closest scores a point for each of their stones that's nearer than the other team's closest stone. A match is played over multiple ends — CurlingOne defaults to 10.

The team that didn't score last gets the hammer — the advantage of throwing the final stone — in the next end, which is what keeps close games interesting all the way through.

CurlingOne implements the real restrictions that keep the game strategic rather than just a takeout contest: the 5-rock rule and no-tick rule in Standard, and a 3-rock rule power-play in Doubles, where the skip decides whether to replace or leave affected stones. Doubles also gives you switch-order and power-play options between ends. You don't need to memorize these going in — the game prompts you when they apply.

06Playing with friends

CurlingOne has no public matchmaking — there's no lobby list of strangers to join. Instead, you and the people you want to play with agree on a room name beforehand (over text, Discord, wherever you already talk), then one person creates the room and everyone else joins using that same name.

Voice chat is on automatically once you're in a multiplayer match with another player present, so you can call shots and talk strategy like you would on real ice. It's off in Single Player.

07Playing solo

Prefer to play alone, or just want to practice? Single Player puts you up against a full AI team, with five skill levels from Very Low to Very High — so it's a fair fight whether you're learning the ropes or looking for a real challenge.
